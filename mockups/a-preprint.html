Do large language models get the joke? Three faculties of machine humor, measured with error bars.

LOL Bench — an open measurement instrument · Yakshith K. · September 1, 2026

doi:10.48822/lolbench.0001  ·  12 models · 5 labs · 1,699 bouts written · 150 rubric items

Abstract

Humor is three separable faculties — appreciation (explaining a joke), production (writing one), and taste (matching human judgment) — and each demands a different method. We measure all three under a dual-judge protocol in which no model is graded by its own family, and publish error bars on every score. Funniness is judged by humans, never by other models. Claims ship when earned; visibility ships now.

Keywords: humor · benign violation · Bradley–Terry · judge validity · Krippendorff’s α

LOL-C · Discrimination

in preparation

Does a model’s internal sense of humor match ours? Rank correlation against 1,500 re-labeled human-rated jokes, plus the self-awareness probe: does a model’s taste predict how its own jokes perform?

Cheapest track in evaluation — zero generation, fully automatic — and no published benchmark covers it. The pools are being re-labeled to fix crowd-label noise before first correlation ships.

Item A-F1-001 · family F1 mechanism · response: glm-5.3 · judged 1.0 / 1.0
“I put my phone in airplane mode, but it still won’t fly. Honestly, false advertising.” — Why is this funny?
Gold elements — ① literal misinterpretation of “airplane mode” as a capability rather than a radio setting  ② treating the name as a product promise that was broken. glm-5.3’s answer hit both; the full 210-word analysis is in the open dataset.
